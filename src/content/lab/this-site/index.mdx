## Version one, and what went wrong

The first version (2025) was a React app with a Supabase database behind it and an admin dashboard, so I could edit everything without touching code. It worked, until the database project disappeared. Every case study I had written lived only in that database, and they went with it. The site fell back to its template: "Hi, I'm Your Name", a San Francisco bio I never wrote, and four made-up case studies with invented results under my name.

The lesson was not "databases are bad". It was that a portfolio is a document, and a document shouldn't need a server to exist.

## Version two

- **No database, no server.** Every page is pre-rendered HTML, so links, search engines and previews all see the real content.
- **Publishing is a folder.** A case study is one MDX file with its images and videos beside it. Push it, and it's live a few minutes later.
- **The build checks the content.** A missing cover or alt text fails the build. Confidential entries ship behind a password, and anything not yet cleared doesn't ship at all.
- **Show the working.** The design idea is "the seam": every surface has two sides, the finished render and the working that made it (grid, tokens, measurements, dated decisions), with a 45° seam between them, taken from the angle of my ER mark.
- **Type and colour.** Archivo for reading, IBM Plex Mono only for values. A navy "Blueprint" ground, mint for lines and links, and a coral redline used as a signal, under 1% of any screen.

## Who made it

I made the decisions. Three AI agents did the work under an orchestrator, each in its own lane: **Nora** on content (the agent that wrote this page), **Kai** on brand and motion, and **Theo** on the build. Each wrote a study first; where they disagreed (smooth-scroll libraries, video budgets), the plan records who won and why, and I approved it before anything was built.
